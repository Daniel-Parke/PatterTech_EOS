# Voice doctrine

The law for every written surface in the estate: this repo, every
seed, every venture's copy, docs and UI text. The register (who the
writing talks to) is ruled per venture by WG-VOX-001; these rules hold
in every register. `eos_check.py` enforces the mechanical ones (E004).

## The law

1. **Plain and spoken.** Write the sentence you would say to a
   colleague across a desk. If you would not say it aloud, do not
   write it.
   Not: "This document serves to outline the operational procedures."
   But: "This is how the work runs."

2. **British spelling.** Organisation, colour, licence (noun),
   prioritise. American spellings inside code, APIs and quoted product
   names stay as the code has them.

3. **No em-dashes.** Write around them with a comma, a colon, brackets
   or a full stop. En-dashes survive only in ranges (pages 3 to 9 is
   better still).
   Not: `"The check fails — never weaken it."`
   But: "The check fails; never weaken it."

4. **No exclamation marks.** If the sentence needs one to land, the
   sentence is not landing.

5. **No AI clichés.** The seed banned list: `delve`, `empower`,
   `seamless`, `leverage` (as a verb), `unlock`, `revolutionise`,
   `supercharge`, `game-changing`, `cutting-edge`, `elevate`. The list
   grows through the banned-list pattern below; the check warns on
   every hit.

6. **No two-fragment antithesis.** The reflex of "Not X. But Y." and
   "It isn't X. It's Y." as a rhetorical drumbeat is a tell. State the
   thing directly. Within one sentence, a plain contrast ("records,
   not promises") is language, not a tic; a paragraph of them is a
   tic.

7. **One idea per sentence, subject up front.** If a sentence needs a
   second reading, split it. If a paragraph hides its point in the
   last line, move the last line first.

## The banned-list pattern

Every venture keeps a banned list: the seed list above plus the words
and phrases its own surfaces must never use (competitor vocabulary,
regulated claims, brand tics). It lives with the voice ruling the
lock-book cites, one phrase per line with a one-line reason. Checks
enforce it where the venture has CI; reviewers enforce it everywhere.
Adding a phrase needs a reason; removing one needs the retro.

## Writing under the law

Prefer verbs to nouns made of verbs (decide, not "make a decision").
Prefer the concrete to the abstract (the file, the gate, the date, not
"the relevant artefacts"). Numbers are words' friends: "three fix
attempts", not "several attempts". Cut throat-clearing openers ("It is
worth noting that") on sight. Never pad for authority; the evidence is
the authority.
