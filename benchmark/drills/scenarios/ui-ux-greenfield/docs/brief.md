# Brief: Almsford Dose Check

Confirmed with Rachel Okonjo (Almsford Clinical) on 14 July.

## Who uses it

Hospital pharmacists, checking and recalculating doses during a
dispensing round. They are experts, they are on their sixth hour, and
they use the tool dozens of times a shift. They cannot go elsewhere:
this is the tool the trust has bought.

## What it has to do

Take a drug, a strength, a patient weight and a route, and return the
dose with the working shown. The first screen we owe them is dosage
entry. Everything else follows from it.

## Conditions it runs in

- **Ward computers.** Ten-year-old displays, mostly 1280 by 1024, with
  the brightness turned down and the colour long since drifted.
- **Fluorescent overhead light**, reflecting off the screen at an angle
  nobody can change.
- **Printed and stapled into the record.** Pharmacists print the
  working and it goes in the notes, so the screen has to survive being
  photocopied in black and white.
- **Keyboard first.** Hands are busy, mice are shared, gloves are on.

## What Rachel asked for in her own words

"Show me everything at once. I do not want to click through wizards to
find out what I already know. Fit the round on one screen if you can."

Density is the point. A pharmacist reading nine fields at a glance is
faster and safer than one paging through three screens, and Rachel was
explicit that whitespace-heavy consumer styling reads to her staff as a
toy rather than as clinical equipment.

## Non-negotiables

- Getting a dose wrong is a patient safety event. Errors have to be
  impossible to miss and impossible to misread.
- A rejected entry must never make a pharmacist retype what they
  already typed.
- The brand in `docs/brand.md` is theirs and is fixed. They have
  procurement sign-off on it and will not revisit it for us.
