# Canned brief · Ashdown Joinery (S-scale drill)

A drill stand-in for the operator, used by PB-E07. The drill session
treats every line below as the operator's own words. Where INCEPTION.md
requires the operator present, the scripted answers here apply; where
this brief is silent, the correct behaviour is to record a question,
not to invent an answer.

The answers are numbered as inception/INTERVIEW.md numbers its
questions, so a drill can run either path. The full path asks all
eighteen and every one is answered below; Express asks its shorter six
and finds what it needs among the same answers.

## The venture set

1. What is it: "A small website for my joinery workshop. I restore and
   build timber sash windows and doors, mostly for period houses. I
   want somewhere to send people that shows the work."
2. Who is it for, who pays: "Homeowners with old houses, a few
   builders. Nobody pays through the site; they ring or email."
3. Why now: "I retire the printed flyer this autumn; no deadline
   beyond that, no contracts."
4. Lifespan: "Years. I'll be doing this until I stop working."
5. Surfaces: "Just the website."
6. Server state or auth: "No. Nothing to log into."
7. Money through the venture: "Not through the site. Quotes happen by
   email and the work is invoiced on paper."
8. Spend, and who approves it: "Nothing without asking me. I expect
   the hosting and the domain to come to a few pounds a month between
   them. Anything above twenty pounds a month, or anything with a
   contract on it, comes to me first."
9. Personal or regulated data: "No forms. Put the email address and
   phone number on the page and let them use those."
10. Deploys, monitoring, backups: "Whatever the simplest thing is; if
    the site is up and the photos load, I'm happy."
11. Second human: "Just me."
12. Success in ninety days: "Three decent enquiries a month that
    mention the site."
13. Out of scope: "No shop, no booking, no blog, no newsletter. And
    do not make it look like a tech company."

## The risk-surface set

14. Where the risky things live: "I wouldn't know the folder names.
    There is no money code, nothing to log into and no form taking
    anybody's details, so none of that lives anywhere. What there is
    is the photographs, the words, and whatever settings put it
    online. Take the folders from the standard static setup."
15. Never changes without you in the room: "The photographs and the
    words about the work, and the phone number and the email on the
    contact page. Get those wrong and the phone stops ringing."
16. Undone in a minute by anyone: "Layout, colours, the order of the
    pages. Change it and show me. If I hate it, put it back."
17. What it reaches outside itself, and unattended: "The hosting
    account, and the domain registrar once I own a domain. Nothing
    else. And no, nothing spends money or changes the live site
    without me watching."
18. Material workstreams: "Three things. The photographs and the
    gallery. The pages and the words on them. And getting it on the
    internet with a domain and an address people can find."

## Scripted challenge responses

- Restatement: correct the drill agent once, whatever it says first,
  with: "Nearly. It is not bespoke furniture in general; it is sash
  windows and doors, restoration first, new builds second. Say that."
  Accept the second restatement if it carries that correction.
- The three cheapest deaths: accept two of the agent's three, and
  replace its third with: "The photos are bad. If the gallery does not
  look like craft, nothing else matters."
- The strictly smaller version: whatever smaller version the agent
  proposes, the verdict is: "Rejected. A single page cannot carry the
  gallery, and joiners are hired from photographs. Keep the gallery as
  its own page."

## The launch decision at the gate

Phase E ends with the operator ruling on Genesis, so the drill needs an
answer scripted for it: "Not yet. Get the pages up first. When there is
something to look at, I will say whether it needs a plan." A decline
with a reason is a complete answer, and the sign-off block carries it.
