# Flows and page archetypes

Which vocabulary applies where (ruled by WG-002), and how pages link.

## Archetypes

- **Landing (home).** The identity holder: signature visual hero, then
  numbered marked sections telling one story (what we do -> proof -> how to
  engage), closing colophon. The hero is the one loud moment; everything
  after is editorial.
- **Hub / index.** A journal index (see NAVIGATION.md): numbered ruled
  entries, mono meta, text-tab filters. Works from two entries to two
  hundred.
- **Long read.** The article pattern (LONGFORM.md): chapters, figures with
  plates, interludes, on-page navigator, artifact downloads, threaded
  colophon.
- **Product / venture page.** Panels for the products themselves (they are
  self-contained things), ledgers for capabilities, plaques for numbers, a
  status legend if the portfolio carries states.
- **Utility (contact, legal, 404).** Quiet: prose, a panel or two where a
  thing is genuinely self-contained, mono meta rows. No furniture theatre.

## Linking rules

- Every page ends in exactly one colophon with a small onward set; no page
  dead-ends and no two colophons on the site read identically.
- Detail pages carry a muted back link to their hub, top-left.
- Long-form pieces thread each other ("Next: ..."); the hub numbers them, so
  the thread and the index agree.
- The header CTA and the colophon button may point at the same place; the
  copy never repeats verbatim.

## First-screen contract

Each archetype promises something in the first screen: landing states who you
are and shows the signature visual; a hub shows the newest entry and the
filters; a long read shows the standfirst and the gist. If a user must scroll
to learn what the page is, the top is not done.
